Northside Entertainers Cooperative, Naija

NEC Naija

Tagline: Stronger together. Growing together.

1. About NEC Naija

Northside Entertainers Cooperative, Naija (NEC Naija) is a proposed cooperative for entertainers across Northern Nigeria. It aims to help members build financial strength, access support for projects, coordinate activities and invest collectively in profitable ventures.

Membership may include musicians, comedians, dancers and other entertainment professionals. NEC Naija will operate centrally and oversee state chapters, each managed by a committee responsible for cooperative activities in its state.

2. Membership and fees

At the proposed October 2026 launch, the membership fee is ₦50,000, payable through necnaija.com. Applicants must complete their online membership profile to be recognised as full members.

The fee is renewable annually and is non-refundable. It is intended to cover the cooperative’s administrative and operating expenses.

3. Member contributions

Members are expected to contribute ₦10,000 each month. They may also make additional contributions at any time, in any amount, if they have funds they wish to add to their cooperative contribution balance, which would affect their profit sharing margin, during profit sharing.

The NEC Naija web app and application will record member contributions, loans, repayments and requests.

4. Annual requests and contribution rollover

At the end of each year, following the declaration of the cooperative’s profits and losses, members will be able to submit an annual request through their NEC Naija account. Members may request:

- Their allocated profits only; or
- Their allocated profits and their contributions.

Members who choose to carry their contributions into the following year will receive a 10% bonus on those contributions, credited to their contribution balance in the system. They may request that balance, together with their profits, during the following year’s annual request period.

5. Profit sharing and reserves

At least 70% of the cooperative’s profits will be allocated for distribution among members at the end of each year. The distribution will be based on each member’s contribution recorded in the system.

For clarity, NEC Naija should state in its formal rules exactly how each member’s share is calculated, for example, by comparing a member’s eligible contribution balance with the total eligible contribution balances, and the date on which balances are measured.

The remaining 30% will be retained as a reserve for future investments and activities intended to support the cooperative’s growth. Annual reports will set out the cooperative’s profits, losses, reserves and opportunities for the coming year.

6. Loans and repayment

Members may apply for loans to support projects or businesses. Applications may be reviewed using credit checks and, depending on the amount, guarantors or collateral.

Loans must be repaid before December of each year, under the repayment terms agreed when the loan is issued. If a member defaults, NEC Naija will seek repayment through the agreed guarantor or collateral arrangements. Any recovery process should follow the relevant agreements and applicable requirements.

For a small loan issued without a guarantor or collateral, an unpaid balance may be recorded as a cooperative loss for that year. Management will continue to pursue repayment and report the default to members through the NEC Naija web app or application.

7. Digital records and reporting

The NEC Naija web app and application will track contributions, loans, repayments and member requests. The system is intended to provide members with a clear record of cooperative activity and support transparent administration.

Management will publish quarterly operational reports through the web app and application. At the end of each year, it will publish an annual report covering performance, profits, losses, reserves and potential opportunities for the next year.

8. Ownership and voting

NEC Naija is proposed to be owned and managed by stakeholders who buy into the cooperative’s ownership before launch.

The proposed ownership value is ₦100,000,000, divided into 100 ownership units of ₦1,000,000 each. The founder, Mr Jethro Mark Da’ar, is assigned 10% ownership and may purchase additional ownership units at ₦1,000,000 per unit. Which leaves only 90% ownership stake left to be claimed.

The Owners’ Membership Board will have final authority over cooperative decisions. Members may be consulted on proposed ventures and financial decisions, but final approval will rest with the Board.

9. Annual management elections

Cooperative management will be elected or rotated annually, with members able to campaign for management positions. State chapter management committees will follow the same annual election or rotation process.

The Owners’ Membership Board will remain unchanged under the proposed structure.

10. State chapters and event coordination

Members must submit proposals for shows, concerts, rallies and other events at least three months before the planned date. The relevant state chapter will review each proposal, compare it with other submitted events and assess potential clashes. Approval is expected to take 14 - 20 days.

If events are likely to compete for the same audience, the chapter may work with the organisers to adjust a date or venue. This coordination is intended to give members room to promote their events and to encourage entertainers to attend and support one another’s shows, concerts, releases and other activities.
